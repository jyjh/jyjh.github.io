## Load Transfer - Steady State


We start with the most basic concept of load transfer. This can be derived quite simply from basic physics principles.

We begin with the translational accelerations. Longitutudinal and lateral: forwards and sideways.
The only point of contact a vehicle has with the ground is the tires. Thus the force for these accelerations will always come from the contact patches.
The center of mass of a vehicle is also always above the contact patch of the tires. Otherwise, portions of the vehicle would have to be underground. Thus these forces will always create a torque.
During a steady state acceleration, the torque must be cancelled out, since the vehicle would otherwise be accelerating rotationally, which contradicts a steady state scenario.

Thus, the normal forces of the tires must be distributed in a way that "cancels' this torque. Equally, the normal forces of the tires must sum to the mass of the car. (Otherwise the car would be accelerating vertically and thus flying.)
